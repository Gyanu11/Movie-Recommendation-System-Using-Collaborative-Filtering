About

Movie Hub

Movie Hub is a Flask web application that recommends films using item-based collaborative filtering trained on the MovieLens 20M dataset. Rather than comparing plot summaries or genres, it compares how real people rated things: two movies are similar when the same viewers tended to rate them the same way.

How the algorithm works

  1. 1. Filter. Movies with fewer than 500 ratings are dropped. Similarity computed from a handful of ratings is noise, not signal.
  2. 2. Build the matrix. The surviving ratings become a sparse movie × user matrix, where each cell is the score one user gave one film.
  3. 3. Adjust. Each movie's mean rating is subtracted from its own row. This removes the bias of films that almost everyone likes, leaving only how much more or less than usual each viewer enjoyed it.
  4. 4. Cosine similarity. Every row is normalised to unit length, so multiplying the matrix by its own transpose yields the cosine of the angle between every pair of movies — a score from 0 to 1.
  5. 5. Serve. Only the top 50 neighbours of each movie are kept, shrinking the model from about 80 MB to under 2 MB. Recommending then costs a dictionary lookup and a short sum, not a matrix multiplication.

Personal recommendations

For a signed-in user, every movie you have rated contributes its neighbours to a running score, weighted by how far your rating sat above or below the middle of the scale. A five-star rating pulls its neighbours up; a one-star rating pushes them down. Films you have already rated or saved are removed before the ranking is returned.

Dataset

MovieLens 20M, published by the GroupLens research group at the University of Minnesota. It contains 20,000,263 ratings and 465,564 tag applications across 27,278 movies from 138,493 users.
